Internal Communications

How team emails, internal meetings, and inter-team messages are handled. Same machinery, different routing.

Same Pipeline, Different Destination

Internal communications - Eddie emailing you about a client issue, a team sync meeting, Reileigh messaging about a deadline - go through the same ingestion and processing pipeline as everything else. TrashMan filters junk. The Communication agent reads, categorizes, and decides what to do.

The only difference is where things land. The Communication agent asks one additional question for internal messages: is this about a client, or is this about us?

Example: Three Internal Messages, Three Routes

Eddie emails you: "Hey, Stone & Tile's landing page has a broken form. I'm going to fix it today." This is about a client. It goes on Stone & Tile's timeline, attributed to Eddie. If it needs action from you, it becomes a task.

Reileigh emails you: "Can we push our team meeting to 3pm?" This is about the business, not a client. It goes on the internal timeline. Probably doesn't need a task - just context.

Team sync meeting: You discuss Stone & Tile's campaign, Nzymes' onboarding progress, and hiring a new designer. The Stone & Tile items go on Stone & Tile's timeline. The Nzymes items go on Nzymes' timeline. The hiring discussion goes on the internal timeline. One meeting, three destinations.

The Internal Timeline

Client timelines track what happens with each client. The internal timeline tracks what happens inside the agency itself - team decisions, operational changes, hiring, process updates, internal projects.

It follows the exact same guarantees as client timelines:

  • Append-only - entries are never edited or deleted
  • Chronological - ordered by time
  • Attributed - every entry records who or what created it
  • Linked to source - meeting recordings, email threads, original documents
  • Corrections, not edits - mistakes are corrected by appending, not rewriting

Right now, the internal timeline is one stream for the whole agency. As the team grows and the business gets more complex, it can split into domain-specific timelines:

Timeline What Goes Here When to Split
Internal (general) Everything that isn't client-specific. Team decisions, process changes, general operations. V1 - this is the starting point
Marketing Internal marketing strategy, content planning, brand discussions When marketing becomes its own function with its own team
Development Product development, tool building, technical decisions When dev work is frequent enough to need its own history
Fulfillment Client delivery operations, capacity planning, workload management When fulfillment coordination is a daily activity
HR Hiring, onboarding team members, performance, training When the team is large enough to have HR as a function
Design Principle
Start with one internal timeline. Split when it becomes hard to find things because there's too much unrelated information mixed together. The split should be driven by practical need, not organizational theory. If you never need to separate marketing from development, don't.

Internal Meetings

Internal meetings go through the same processing pipeline as client meetings - recorded, transcribed, action items extracted, batch review. The only difference is routing.

Meeting Type Processing Where It Lands
Client meeting
(call with Ruthy at Nzymes)		 Summary + action items extracted. Batch review. Summary and action items go on Nzymes' timeline.
Internal meeting, client-focused
(team sync about Stone & Tile strategy)		 Same processing. Summary + action items. Goes on Stone & Tile's timeline, attributed as internal discussion.
Internal meeting, multi-topic
(weekly team sync covering 3 clients + hiring)		 Same processing. Items attributed per topic. Client items go on respective client timelines. Non-client items go on internal timeline.
Internal meeting, no clients
(hiring discussion, process review)		 Same processing. Summary + action items. Everything goes on the internal timeline.
Example: Weekly Team Sync

Friday team sync covers: Stone & Tile's budget issue (5 min), Nzymes onboarding status (3 min), ExclusiveMatchmaking's new campaign idea (4 min), and hiring a junior designer (8 min).

The meeting agent produces one summary and seven action items. It attributes each to the right destination:

  • Stone & Tile summary + 2 action items -> Stone & Tile's timeline
  • Nzymes update + 1 action item -> Nzymes' timeline
  • ExclusiveMatchmaking idea + 2 action items -> ExclusiveMatchmaking's timeline
  • Hiring discussion + 2 action items -> Internal timeline

Each timeline entry links back to the recording at the relevant timestamp. The Nzymes entry links to 5:12 in the recording, not the beginning.

Team Emails and Messages

Emails between team members are ingested through the same pipeline. The Communication agent determines:

  1. Is this about a client? If Eddie emails you "Stone & Tile's form is broken" - that's about a client. It goes on Stone & Tile's timeline and may create a task.
  2. Is this internal operations? If Reileigh emails "Can we move the meeting?" - that's internal. It goes on the internal timeline. Probably no task needed.
  3. Is this noise? If it's "sounds good" or "ok thanks" - it doesn't need to be on any timeline. The agent lets it pass through without recording it.
Key Point
Internal communications are not second-class. They go through the same pipeline, get the same processing, and land on the right timeline with full attribution and source links. The only thing that differs is routing - client timeline vs. internal timeline. The system treats internal work as real work, because it is.

What Doesn't Change

As the team grows and internal communication volume increases:

  • The pipeline stays the same (TrashMan -> Comm Agent -> route to timeline)
  • The processing stays the same (categorize, check context, create task or log)
  • The meeting processing stays the same (transcribe, extract, attribute, batch review)
  • The timeline guarantees stay the same (append-only, chronological, attributed, linked)

What changes: the internal timeline may split into multiple timelines as the business gets more complex. And the Communication agent gets better at routing as it sees more internal communication patterns. But the architecture doesn't change - just the number of timelines it can write to.
