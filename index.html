01 Operating Principles

Picture this: it's 7:15 AM. You open one app. Not Gmail, not Meta Ads Manager, not a project tracker, not Stripe. One app. Here's what you see - and more importantly, here's what you don't have to go looking for.

Everything in this system flows from eight rules. They're not guidelines. They're the bones of the thing. Break one and the whole architecture stops making sense.

  1. One app, one task queue. No more toggling between Gmail, Google Ads, and a spreadsheet to figure out what needs doing today. Stone & Tile Shoppe's budget alert, Ruthy from Nzymes asking about CPA, the ExclusiveMatchmaking onboarding steps - they all live in the same sidebar, sorted by what matters most right now.
  2. Everything is a conversation thread. Every piece of work is a task. Every task is a chat with the AI. You talk to it like a team member who already has all the context. Two exceptions: a bird's-eye client overview and a monitoring rule management view. Everything else is conversation.
  3. Pull-based, not push-based. Tasks appear in the sidebar. You work them when ready. No notifications, no badges, no anxiety-inducing red dots - except for genuine emergencies. TheRealSleep's website going down at 2 AM? That pushes. Ruthy's non-urgent question about next month's budget? It waits for you.
  4. All client communication requires human approval. The AI drafts, you send. Nzymes gets a perfectly-worded email about their Q2 strategy - but only after you've read it and hit send. No automated messages go to clients. Ever.
  5. Monitoring rules are deterministic thresholds, not AI judgment. "Fire when Stone & Tile's frequency exceeds 3.0" - that's a rule. Not "notice if things seem off." Every rule fires on a specific, predictable condition. The AI helps fix the issue once detected, but detection itself is a concrete threshold you set.
  6. Admin watches and alerts. It never acts. ExclusiveMatchmaking's invoice is 15 days overdue? Admin tells you. It tells Reporting to hold the cheerful weekly update. But it never sends a payment reminder, never pauses a campaign, never contacts the client about money. That's yours.
  7. Every action is logged to the client timeline. Eddie sent Nzymes an email from Gmail on his phone? Logged. Monitoring auto-fixed Stone & Tile's budget overspend at 3 AM? Logged. You approved a weekly report for TheRealSleep? Logged. One chronological record per client. Nothing is lost. Nothing is siloed.
  8. The system fails visibly, never silently. When something goes wrong - an API is down, an auto-fix didn't work, a merge was wrong - you see it. Clear language, clear options. No swallowed errors, no silent retries you never hear about.

What Is a "Client"?

A client is a company the agency works with - Stone & Tile Shoppe, Nzymes, ExclusiveMatchmaking, TheRealSleep. Each client can have multiple contacts (the CEO, the marketing manager, accounts payable). When Ruthy from Nzymes sends an email, it's attributed to Ruthy as the contact. But the timeline belongs to Nzymes as a whole - one timeline per company, so anyone working the account sees the full picture.

03 Five Domains at a Glance

Five agents constantly watching the outside world. Each one knows how to find work, create tasks, and hand them to you in a unified queue. You never think about which domain you're in. You just see what needs doing.

COMM Communication
Ruthy from Nzymes texts at 11 PM, Stone & Tile's owner emails a question, ExclusiveMatchmaking sends a WhatsApp voice note. This domain catches all of it - email, SMS, WhatsApp, meetings - filters the junk, and turns the rest into tasks ready for you to handle.
MON Monitoring
Stone & Tile's Google Ads budget is pacing 35% over. TheRealSleep's landing page is throwing 503 errors. Nzymes' Facebook frequency hit 3.2. This domain watches every ad account, every website, every conversion pixel - and either fixes it automatically or asks you what to do.
SETUP Setup & Actions
New client just signed? This domain walks you through every step: billing setup, ad account access, conversion tracking, creative production, campaign launch. Structured workflows for known processes, open AI workspace for anything novel.
RPT Timeline & Reporting
Keeps the memory of every client alive. Maintains timelines, drafts periodic updates ("Here's how Nzymes did this month"), follows up when clients owe you assets or approvals. The client-facing communication arm - but only with your approval.
ADM Administration
ExclusiveMatchmaking's invoice is 15 days overdue. TheRealSleep's contract expires in 60 days. This domain watches billing, payments, and contracts. It alerts you and tells Reporting to adjust its tone. But it never acts on money - that's yours.

Each domain is covered in detail in the Domain Deep-Dives sections below. But first - the task system that ties them all together. Understanding this makes everything else click.

04 The Task System: One Type, One Queue

Here's the core insight: it doesn't matter if the work came from an email, a monitoring alert, or a billing issue. It's all a task. It's all a chat thread. And it's all in the same sidebar. The source is just metadata - a tag that tells you where it came from, not a separate place you need to go.

What a Task Contains

When Ruthy from Nzymes emails about her CPA, or when Monitoring catches Stone & Tile's budget overspend, the resulting task always has the same shape:

Tasks as Chat Threads

Here's the magic. You click "Stone & Tile - Budget Pacing" in the sidebar, and you're in a conversation with an AI that already knows: Stone & Tile's timeline for the past 30 days, their active campaigns, their budget allocation, what the monitoring rule detected, and what it already did about it. The AI has preprocessed everything before you opened the thread.

What Preprocessing Produces

Before you even click, the AI has gathered context based on what kind of task it is:

  • Communication task - Ruthy's email plus her recent message history, active tasks for Nzymes, and a suggested reply draft or summary of what she needs
  • Monitoring (auto-fix) - what was detected ("budget pacing 35% over"), what was done ("reduced daily cap from $85 to $62"), and current metric state
  • Monitoring (ask-permission) - what was detected, the proposed fix with reasoning, and historical data so you can decide
  • Meeting action items - batch of items with transcript excerpts and recording links at the exact timestamp
  • Admin task - billing/contract data, relevant history, and suggested next steps

Cross-Domain Tool Access

Imagine you're working an Admin task - ExclusiveMatchmaking's invoice is overdue. You decide to email them about it. You don't need to leave the task and go to a "communications" section. The AI surfaces Communication tools right there in the thread. You say "draft a gentle reminder about the invoice," it drafts the email, you approve, it sends. Admin created the task; Communication tools helped you resolve it. The AI surfaces tools based on what the conversation needs, not which domain created the task.

Priority Escalation Over Time

Things that sit too long don't just disappear - they get louder. Ruthy's email goes unanswered for 24+ hours? It escalates from Normal to High. A monitoring ask-permission task where the condition is getting worse? Normal to High. The principle is simple: aging problems should not stay invisible. Configurable rules control the timing, but the idea is baked in.

User Override

You can change any task's priority. If you bump Ruthy's email down to Low because you know she's on vacation and it's not urgent, that override is sticky - the system won't re-escalate it. The system does not fight you.

Staleness

If a task sits unworked for 48+ hours, it re-preprocesses when you finally open it. You always see current information, not stale data from two days ago.

Archive

Completed tasks are permanently searchable by client, date, keyword, or domain. Need to find that conversation with Ruthy about CPA from three months ago? It's there. A "recently completed" section provides quick access to the last few days.

Deduplication

Here's a real scenario: Ruthy from Nzymes emails you saying "our leads dropped this week." At the same time, Monitoring independently detects that Nzymes' conversion rate dropped 40%. That's the same problem, noticed from two different directions. You don't want two separate tasks clogging your sidebar - you want one task that shows both the client's concern and the data.

The system merges them into a single task showing both sources. When you open it, you see Ruthy's email and the monitoring data side by side.

Matching

Same client + overlapping timeframe (24 hours) + related issue. Within monitoring rule groups, dedup is deterministic - it knows two budget rules for Stone & Tile are about the same thing. Cross-domain dedup (Ruthy's email + a monitoring alert) uses AI judgment.

When Dedup Is Wrong

  • False merge - the system incorrectly combined two unrelated issues? You can split them back into two tasks, each keeping its original context
  • Missed merge - two tasks that should be one? You can manually merge them

Team & Assignment

Right now, you're the hub. Everything flows through you. The system is designed for that reality while leaving room for the team to take on more.

  • Default - all tasks assigned to Will unless reassigned
  • Manual reassignment - you can reassign from within a thread or from the sidebar. "Eddie, handle the Nzymes creative refresh" - one click.
  • Auto-assignment - future feature. V1 is all manual.

Visibility

You see all tasks across the team. Eddie, Reileigh, Tristan, Isaac - they each see only their assigned tasks. You can filter the sidebar by team member to check on anyone's workload. Bird's-eye view shows who is assigned to what across all clients.

Concurrent Access

Only the assigned owner can send messages and execute tools in a task thread. Others see a read-only live view. If you need to jump in on something Eddie is handling, you reassign it to yourself first. No conflicting actions, no confusion about who's driving.

05 Communication: Inbound Messages to Tasks

It's Monday morning. 47 emails came in over the weekend. Your phone has 3 WhatsApp messages from ExclusiveMatchmaking and Ruthy from Nzymes texted at 11 PM about her CPA numbers. Here's what happens - without you lifting a finger.

The Communication domain ingests everything: email, SMS, WhatsApp, and meeting recordings. It filters the noise, identifies who's talking, figures out what matters, and turns actionable messages into tasks that are waiting for you in the sidebar - already preprocessed with context and draft replies.

Communications Are Continuous
Each individual communication is its own actionable item with a done/not-done state. When you reply to Ruthy's CPA question and mark it done, that means "I've handled this message" - not that you'll never hear from Ruthy again. Her next email is a new item.

Batching

When ExclusiveMatchmaking's owner sends 5 rapid-fire WhatsApp messages in a panic about their ad performance, you don't get 5 tasks. Messages from the same sender within 5 minutes are batched and processed as one unit. One task, full context.

Backpressure

High-volume bursts (inbox backfill after connecting a new team member, spam floods) are queued and processed in order. Messages are never dropped. Historical backfill processes in reverse-chronological order - most recent first, because that's what you care about most.

Outbound Messages

When Eddie sends Nzymes an email from his phone, the system captures it via sent-mail ingestion and logs it to the Nzymes timeline - so everyone has context. When you compose a message from within the app, you ask the AI to draft it, review the draft, approve, and send. Same timeline, same record, regardless of origin.

Attachments & Media

Files (images, PDFs, spreadsheets, voice notes) are stored and linked to the message. If Stone & Tile sends over a new product photo that's relevant to an active creative task, the agent links it there automatically.

TrashMan starts extremely conservative. Only filters what it's absolutely certain is junk. If there's any doubt - any chance it might be from a client, about a client, or relevant to real work - it passes through. A false negative (junk gets through) is annoying. A false positive (a real message gets filtered) could cost a client.

Learning

TrashMan only learns through your explicit approval. When you repeatedly dismiss messages from the same sender - say, those daily SEMrush marketing emails - TrashMan proposes a blacklist entry: "Block all from marketing@semrush.com?" You approve or reject. Once approved, the pattern can extend to similar messages from the same sender. TrashMan never invents new categories on its own. It asks, you decide.

Recovery

Everything filtered is logged to a recovery log. You can browse it anytime and restore anything with a click. Messages from client-associated domains are automatically flagged as potential false positives - if nzymes.com shows up in the filter log, that gets highlighted immediately. Restored messages re-enter the pipeline at the Comm Agent step as if they'd never been filtered.

The agent identifies Ruthy, reads the Nzymes timeline, sees there's no existing task about CPA, detects this is a question needing a response, and creates a HIGH task with Ruthy's email, recent performance data, and a draft reply.

Sender Identification

Matches by email/phone/WhatsApp ID. If ExclusiveMatchmaking's owner emails from a personal address the system hasn't seen before, it's surfaced for manual triage - "Unknown sender, do you know this person?" When you identify them, that address is linked for all future messages.

Thread Matching

Email: thread ID (Reply-To chain). SMS/WhatsApp: same contact within a recent time window. If Stone & Tile replies to a three-month-old email thread about their summer campaign with an unrelated question about billing, the agent detects the topic shift and creates a new task instead of appending to dead context.

Edge Cases

  • Auto-replies / out-of-office - not filtered, logged to timeline. If a task is waiting on that person (you emailed Ruthy and she's OOO), the auto-reply is noted on the blocked task so you know why.
  • Cross-channel duplicates - TheRealSleep's owner emails AND texts about the same issue? First message processed, duplicate linked with a note: "client reached out via multiple channels." One task, not two.
  • Team email - Eddie sends Nzymes a campaign update from Gmail. Logged to the Nzymes timeline for context but doesn't create a task for you unless it reveals a problem.

Meeting Processing

You just finished a 45-minute call with Stone & Tile Shoppe about their Q3 strategy. Halfway through, their owner mentioned wanting to add Facebook ads. At minute 38, you agreed to send them a creative brief by Friday. Without this system, that lives in your head until you forget it. With this system, it's already a task.

After a recorded meeting, the system produces two outputs:

  1. Meeting summary - a narrative of what was discussed, logged to the client timeline(s) immediately. Not a task - it's context. Anyone looking at Stone & Tile's timeline sees "Q3 strategy call: discussed expanding to Facebook, reviewed Google Ads performance, agreed on creative brief deadline."
  2. Action items - extracted tasks, each with: description, AI reasoning for why it's an action item, the transcript excerpt where it was mentioned, and a recording link at the exact timestamp so you can hear the original conversation.

Batch Review

Action items enter as pending. They're presented as a batch for you to approve, reject, or edit. If the meeting produced 8 items, you can approve 3 that are real, reject 2 that were just brainstorming, and edit the rest. Partial review is fully supported - the unapproved items stay pending. Items that sound identical to ones from last week's meeting are flagged as potential duplicates.

Client Attribution

Multi-client meetings: items are attributed to the correct client. If you discuss Stone & Tile and Nzymes in the same internal sync, each action item goes to the right client timeline. Prospective clients not yet in the system are tagged "unassigned" for you to associate during review.

Quality Gate

Bad transcript quality? Flagged for manual review rather than guessing at action items from garbled audio. The system does not guess. Meetings with no action items still produce a summary - context is always valuable even when there's no immediate work.

All Meetings
Client calls, internal syncs with Eddie and the team, vendor conversations - no meeting type is excluded. An internal sync where someone says "we need to send Nzymes their Q2 report" generates the same action item as a client call.

06 Monitoring: Rules, Thresholds, and Auto-Fixes

It's 3 AM. Stone & Tile Shoppe's Google Ads is burning through budget at 2x the normal rate because a broad match term went haywire. Nobody's awake. The system detects it, reduces the daily cap, adds the offending term as a negative, and has a full report waiting in your sidebar when you wake up. That's Monitoring.

Here's what makes this different from "AI watching your accounts": every rule is a specific, predictable threshold. Not "let the AI decide if things look off." You define the condition, you define what happens when it fires. The AI helps resolve the issue once detected, but detection itself is deterministic - no surprises.

Two Trigger Types

Action-Based

Fire when an event occurs. TheRealSleep's website throws a 5xx error - alert fires immediately.

  • Website 5xx error
  • Ad disapproved
  • Budget exhausted before noon
Time-Based

Fire on a schedule to check. Every morning at 7 AM: are all client sites up?

  • 7 AM: all sites up?
  • Every 6h: frequency below 3.0?
  • Weekly: search term quality

What Gets Monitored

CategoryChecks
Google AdsSearch terms (Stone & Tile getting clicks for "wholesale tile"?), budget pacing, conversions, geo-targeting, disapprovals
Facebook AdsFrequency/fatigue (Nzymes' audience seeing the same ad 4x?), disapprovals, delivery, conversions, cost trends
WebsitesUptime (TheRealSleep's site down?), SSL, page speed, critical errors
Email CampaignsOpen/click/bounce rates, deliverability
Conversion HealthEvents flowing, pixels firing, data gaps (ExclusiveMatchmaking's form submissions stopped registering?)

Each rule has a threshold with system defaults, overridable per client. Stone & Tile might tolerate a frequency of 3.5; ExclusiveMatchmaking might need to stay below 2.0. Full default list is a separate config document.

Auto-Fix vs. Ask-Permission

Every rule has a setting: can the system fix it automatically, or does it need to ask you first? Same category can have both types. Each rule also has a severity (low/med/high/critical).

  • Auto-fix - detects, fixes, reports. For unambiguous situations. Stone & Tile's budget pacing 35% over? Reduce the cap. No judgment call needed. The task in your sidebar is informational: "Here's what I did and why."
  • Ask-permission - detects, proposes a fix with reasoning, waits for you. Nzymes' CPA doubled but it might be seasonal? The system shows you the data, suggests pausing the underperforming ad set, and waits for your call.

If an auto-fix fails (API error, rate limit), it escalates to ask-permission. Low-severity auto-fix results batch into a daily summary so your sidebar isn't cluttered with minor wins. Medium+ appear as individual tasks. Circuit breaker rules governing auto-fix limits (cooldown, max fixes, contradiction handling) are in Section 12.

Data Freshness

Ad platform APIs lag 3-24 hours. Rules check data freshness before firing. If the data is stale, the check is deferred rather than triggering a false alert. No waking you up about a "conversion drop" that's really just a reporting delay.

Rule Lifecycle

Rules aren't static - they need to adapt to what's happening with each client:

EventBehavior
New clientGrace period (default 2 weeks). Stone & Tile just launched? Monitoring collects baseline data, doesn't fire alerts on normal fluctuations. Genuine critical issues (site down) still fire immediately.
Planned restructureYou're rebuilding Nzymes' campaign structure this week. Rules paused for that client with an optional expiry date. No false alarms during the overhaul.
Client offboardedRules auto-deactivated. No zombie alerts.
Missed check (downtime)System was down for maintenance? Runs missed checks on restart and notes the gap in the timeline.

Rule Management

A filterable rule management view (separate from chat) lets you browse, edit, toggle, and create rules across clients. This is the one exception to "everything is a chat thread" for Monitoring - hundreds of rules need a list view, not conversation. You need to see at a glance that Stone & Tile has 14 active rules and Nzymes has 8.

Proposing New Rules

The system can propose new rules, but only during an existing workflow - not by scanning accounts unprompted. If you're working a task about ExclusiveMatchmaking's rising CPAs and the AI notices they don't have a frequency cap rule, it'll suggest one. You approve every parameter before it goes live.

07 Setup & Actions: Onboarding to Launch

A new client just signed. TheRealSleep wants Google Ads, Facebook Ads, and conversion tracking on their Shopify store. Today, that means a mental checklist, scattered notes, and hoping you don't forget to install the pixel before launching the campaign. Tomorrow, it's a structured workflow where each step validates the one before it.

Dedicated Workflows

Structured multi-step processes for things you do regularly: onboarding, campaign launch, conversion tracking setup, ad creation, website design. Each step has a completion condition and can validate the work.

Open Workspace

Interactive AI session for the weird stuff. ExclusiveMatchmaking has a custom CRM that needs a webhook integration? That's a workspace conversation. Unusual tech stacks, new platforms, custom integrations. Permanent first-class feature, not an afterthought.

09 Administration: The Financial Watchdog

ExclusiveMatchmaking's invoice is 15 days overdue. Their contract renews in 60 days. And Stone & Tile's ad spend is pacing 20% under budget for the month. You need to know all of this - but you don't need a system that sends passive-aggressive payment reminders on your behalf. Admin watches. Admin alerts. Admin never acts.

What Admin Watches

  • Invoice status and payment receipts - "Nzymes paid Invoice #1847 on March 28"
  • Overdue payments - alerts at 7, 15, and 30 days. ExclusiveMatchmaking at 7 days is a gentle nudge to you; at 30 days it's a red flag.
  • Contract expiration - alerts at 90, 60, and 30 days. Plenty of runway to have the renewal conversation.
  • Rate changes or billing discrepancies - something doesn't match? You hear about it.

Ad Spend Pacing

Each client has a monthly ad spend budget. The system tracks actual vs. budget continuously. In the bird's-eye view, you see it at a glance: Stone & Tile is green (on pace), Nzymes is yellow (drifting 15% under), TheRealSleep is red (significantly over). Monitoring rules alert when pacing deviates beyond a threshold - and the auto-fix can adjust daily caps before things get out of hand.

How Issues Surface

Admin does two things when it detects an issue. First, it creates a task for you - "ExclusiveMatchmaking invoice 15 days overdue." Second, it notifies the Reporting agent so it adjusts client communications. If ExclusiveMatchmaking's payment is overdue, Reporting knows not to send them a cheerful "What a great month!" update. The domains talk to each other so you don't have to juggle the context yourself.

Guardrails

Cross-domain queries are powerful, but they need boundaries:

  • Read-only - agents query each other for information, not tell each other to act. Monitoring doesn't tell Comm to send an email.
  • Query budget - limits per task to prevent runaway costs. Ruthy's routine email about scheduling doesn't need to query all 4 domains. The system is smart about when cross-queries add value.
  • One level deep - no circular chains. If Comm queries Monitoring, Monitoring does not query back to Comm for that same task.
  • Graceful degradation - if a query fails (timeout, API error), preprocessing proceeds with what it has and notes what's missing. "Monitoring data unavailable - proceed with Comm context only."
  • Transparent - you see exactly which agents were consulted in the preprocessing. No black box.

Pre-Flight Checks

Before the Reporting agent sends any client communication - a weekly update, a follow-up email, anything - it runs four checks:

  1. Any open critical/high tasks for this client? (ExclusiveMatchmaking has a site outage - maybe don't send the cheerful report.)
  2. Any billing issues flagged by Admin? (Their invoice is 15 days overdue - adjust the tone.)
  3. Any active monitoring problems? (Conversions dropped 40% yesterday - don't brag about performance.)
  4. Any angry/escalating communications recently? (The owner sent a frustrated email this morning - read the room.)

If any check is positive, Reporting modifies the message, defers it, or flags it for you to decide. This prevents the "cheerful update to an angry client" problem - the kind of tone-deaf communication that erodes trust.

12 Safety, Errors & Guardrails

A system this autonomous needs hard limits. What happens when the auto-fix creates a new problem? When the AI misclassifies a message? When an API goes down mid-operation? Every failure mode has an answer, and the answer is always: tell the user what happened and give them clear options.

Monitoring Circuit Breakers

Imagine this: Monitoring detects Stone & Tile's budget is overspending and auto-reduces it from $85 to $62. Twenty minutes later, a different rule detects that now it's underspending. Without circuit breakers, the system would keep seesawing - fix, trigger, fix, trigger. Here's what stops that:

  • Cooldown - after an auto-fix, no further auto-fixes on the same entity for 1 hour (configurable). That underspend detection? It becomes an ask-permission task instead of another auto-fix.
  • Max fixes - no more than 3 auto-fixes on the same entity in 24 hours. After 3, everything becomes ask-permission. The system is telling you: "I've tried 3 times, you should look at this."
  • Contradiction detection - two rules with opposing actions (one says pause the campaign, another says increase the budget)? Neither auto-executes. Creates one ask-permission task showing the conflict: "These two rules disagree - here's both sides, you decide."
